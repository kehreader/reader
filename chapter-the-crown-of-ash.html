Edited Outcome

Chapter One

The king died before dawn.

By sunrise, three armies were marching toward the capital.

Rowan stood alone beneath the black banners of House Veyr, staring at the crown his father had worn for thirty-one years.

It was smaller than he remembered.

That surprised him.

As a child, he had imagined the crown as something enormous—golden, magnificent, impossible to lift.

Now it looked like what it truly was:

a circle of blackened metal,

a prison disguised as a symbol.

“Your Highness.”

Lord Marrec stood behind him.

“Put it on.”

Rowan did not move.

Beyond the doors, thousands of voices filled the square. Some shouted his name. Others shouted for his death.

“How many?” Rowan asked.

Marrec hesitated. “How many what?”

“Soldiers.”

“Enough.”

Rowan turned. “That isn’t an answer.”

“It is the only one that matters.”

Rowan looked back at the throne.

There was blood beneath it.

Not his father’s.

Someone else’s.

He crouched and touched the stone. A thin red line disappeared beneath the throne’s base.

His father had once told him something strange.

The crown belongs to whoever is willing to pay for it.

Rowan had thought it was a metaphor.

Now he wasn’t so sure.

A bell sounded in the distance.

Once.

Twice.

Then the palace doors exploded inward.

The guards drew their swords.

Marrec stepped forward.

“Take the crown.”

Rowan looked at it.

For the first time, he understood what his father had meant.

The crown did not belong to the king.

The king belonged to the crown.

Developmental Editing Outcome

The opening has been tightened for stronger pacing, clearer visual progression, and a more immediate sense of political danger. The chapter now establishes three central tensions:

  • Rowan’s reluctance to inherit the throne
  • The military threat gathering outside the capital
  • The crown’s possible connection to blood, power, and supernatural influence

The revised version preserves the original dark-fantasy tone while improving sentence rhythm, reducing repetition, and sharpening the final revelation. The image of the blood beneath the throne has been clarified so it functions as both a literal mystery and a thematic warning. The ending remains unchanged in substance but lands with greater force because the preceding paragraphs build more directly toward Rowan’s realization.
