The Map Beneath the School

Fictional Author: Maya Sterling
Manuscript: 68,000 words
Editing Type: Developmental + Line Editing

```

Chapter One

Alex Mercer had three rules for surviving Westbridge Academy:

  1. Never volunteer.
  2. Never sit in the front row.
  3. And never, under any circumstances, stay after the final bell.

He’d learned the first rule in September, when he answered a question he wasn’t supposed to know and spent the rest of the week being called on in every class.

He learned the second after a month of being “accidentally” chosen by teachers who liked eye contact a little too much.

The third rule, however, was the most important.

Because Westbridge Academy changed after hours.

Not officially, of course. The teachers would laugh if anyone said that aloud. The security guards would blame it on imagination. The principal would call it “adjustment fatigue.”

But the students knew better.

Alex knew better.

Unfortunately, on Thursday afternoon, he broke all three rules at once.

It started with a pencil.

Or, more specifically, with Mrs. Carter catching him drawing a half-finished spaceship battle in the margins of his history textbook.

“Mr. Mercer,” she said, appearing beside his desk as if summoned from thin air, “are we finding the Industrial Revolution boring?”

“No,” Alex said quickly. “I mean—yes. I mean—no, I just—”

“Detention,” she said calmly.

That was rule one broken.

Rule two happened immediately afterward.

Every seat in the middle rows was taken, and the only empty space was in the front row, directly beneath the projector that always flickered as if it were trying to communicate in Morse code.

Alex sat down.

The projector flickered once.

Then twice.

He ignored it.

Rule three came quietly, almost politely.

The final bell rang.

Students surged out of the classroom, chairs scraping, backpacks swinging, voices rising in relief. Within minutes, the hallway outside Room 14 emptied into silence.

Alex stayed behind to pack his things.

He didn’t notice the time passing.

He didn’t notice the building growing quieter.

He noticed only when he finally stood and realized how different the air felt.

He looked around.

The classroom was empty now.

Completely empty.

Even the noise from outside had faded.

“Okay,” he said softly. “That’s… not great.”

He left the room.

The hallway stretched longer than it should have.

The lights buzzed overhead—too steady, too bright—as if they were trying to hide something in plain sight.

Alex walked faster.

His backpack was still beneath his desk.

He only had to retrieve it and leave.

That was all.

Room 14 was at the end of the corridor.

He reached it and pushed open the door.

The room was exactly as it should have been.

Too exactly.

The chairs were aligned. The papers lay perfectly still. The air didn’t move.

His backpack sat beneath his desk.

But something else sat beside it.

A folded piece of paper.

Alex frowned.

He didn’t remember seeing it there.

He knelt and pulled it out.

The moment he touched it, he knew it wasn’t ordinary paper.

It was too thin, too fragile—like it had been waiting a long time to be found.

He unfolded it carefully.

A map.

The school was drawn in perfect detail, but not the kind of detail a student would create. This was precise. Intentional. Almost architectural.

Room 14 was marked.

The library.

The gym.

The cafeteria.

All correct.

Then Alex saw it.

The east wing extended farther than it should have.

Beyond the brick wall every student knew was solid, a corridor appeared in faint ink.

And at the end of it—

A room.

No name.

No label.

Just a shape that felt wrong to look at for too long.

Alex’s throat tightened.

“That’s impossible,” he whispered.

He looked up at the wall.

Brick. Paint. Reality.

Then he looked back at the map.

The room was still there.

Beneath it, a sentence appeared in uneven handwriting, as if the writer had been interrupted halfway through:

If you found this, the school has found you.

Alex’s stomach dropped.

He made his decision instantly.

Leave.

Now.

He folded the map, shoved it into his pocket, grabbed his backpack, and turned toward the door.

That was when the hallway changed.

Footsteps.

Slow.

Deliberate.

Not rushing.

Not wandering.

Approaching.

Alex stopped breathing.

The footsteps halted outside Room 14.

A pause.

Then a whisper, so close it seemed to come from inside his ear.

“Alex.”

He turned slowly toward the door.

The room was empty.

The hallway was empty.

But the silence wasn’t.

It was waiting.

Editing Outcome

The revision establishes a distinctive protagonist voice, improves pacing, adds tension, and turns the map discovery into a stronger narrative hook.
```
