```
Chapter One

Page 217

At 11:37 p.m., Lena Carter finished editing the manuscript.

She leaned back in her chair and rubbed her eyes.

Six hours.

That was how long she'd spent cutting twelve unnecessary pages from a novel that had been far too long to begin with.

The author would probably complain.

Authors always complained.

They loved their words.

Editors were hired to tell them which ones had to disappear.

Lena saved the file.

FINAL_DRAFT_7.docx

She smiled.

"Finally."

Outside her apartment window, rain slid down the glass.

The city below was almost completely dark.

Lena closed the laptop.

She stood.

Then stopped.

Something didn't feel right.

She looked back at the computer.

The screen was still on.

The manuscript had reopened.

Lena frowned.

She hadn't touched the keyboard.

She walked back to the desk.

The page counter at the bottom of the screen read:

217 pages.

She stared.

"No."

The document had contained 216 pages.

She knew that because she had spent the last hour checking the final page numbers.

She clicked the last page.

A new chapter appeared.

There was no title.

Only one sentence.

Lena is reading this.

She laughed.

It was probably a joke from the author.

She scrolled.

The next sentence appeared.

She is sitting at her desk.

Lena's smile disappeared.

She continued reading.

There is a half-empty cup of coffee beside her.

She looked at the cup.

Black coffee.

Two hours old.

Her stomach tightened.

Another line appeared.

The rain has started again.

Lena looked toward the window.

Rain.

She turned back.

The manuscript continued.

She is beginning to understand.

"No."

She closed the document.

The screen went black.

For a moment, there was silence.

Then the computer turned itself back on.

Lena stepped away.

The manuscript opened again.

Page 217.

The cursor blinked beneath the last sentence.

A new line appeared.

She doesn't believe us.

Lena stared at the screen.

"Who are you?"

Nothing happened.

Then:

That isn't the right question.

Lena's heart began beating faster.

She reached for the power cable.

Before she could unplug it, another sentence appeared.

Ask why Chapter Twenty-Eight was removed.

Lena froze.

She knew the manuscript.

There had been twenty-seven chapters.

She had edited every one.

She had never seen Chapter Twenty-Eight.

Her phone buzzed.

She nearly screamed.

A message from the author.

Did you find the missing chapter?

Lena stared at the message.

She typed:

What missing chapter?

The response arrived immediately.

Page 217.

Lena's fingers went cold.

She looked at the screen.

The manuscript was still open.

A new sentence appeared.

He knows you're reading.

Lena looked toward the apartment door.

Nothing.

She walked over and checked the lock.

Locked.

She returned to the desk.

The screen had changed again.

This time there was only one sentence.

At 11:43, someone will knock.

Lena looked at the clock.

11:40.

She laughed nervously.

"This is ridiculous."

11:41.

She sat down.

11:42.

The rain grew louder.

11:43.

Three knocks came from the front door.

Lena didn't move.

Three more.

Then silence.

Her phone buzzed.

The author had sent another message.

Don't open the door.

Lena stared at the message.

Then someone on the other side of the door whispered:

"Lena."

She stopped breathing.

It was her voice.
```

Beta-Reading Report — The Missing Chapter

Beta-Reading Report

The Missing Chapter

Author: Daniel Mercer
Genre: Psychological Thriller / Mystery
```

Overall Impression

The manuscript has one of the strongest high-concept premises in the sample portfolio.

The idea of an editor discovering a manuscript that begins predicting her own life creates an excellent connection between protagonist, profession, and central mystery.

Strongest Elements

Suspense

The gradual progression from a strange page count to increasingly personal predictions works extremely well.

Protagonist

Making Lena an editor is a smart choice because she understands manuscripts and therefore notices irregularities that another protagonist might ignore.

Chapter Ending

The final revelation that the voice outside the door is Lena's own voice is a strong cliffhanger.

Revision Suggestions

The manuscript should avoid revealing too much too quickly.

The mystery should expand through several questions:

  • Who created the manuscript?
  • Why does it know Lena?
  • What happened to Chapter Twenty-Eight?
  • Who is the author really working for?
  • Is Lena changing the story or simply following it?

The middle section should also contain moments where Lena believes she has solved the mystery before discovering she was deliberately misled.

Recommendation

Highly recommended for further development.

The premise is strong enough to support a full thriller if the mystery continues escalating without becoming repetitive.

Beta-reader rating: 9/10
```
