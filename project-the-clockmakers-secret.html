The Clockmaker's Secret

AuthorIsabelle Grant
GenreHistorical Mystery
Length81,000 words
View full before & after (PDF)

Original Manuscript

Before editing — opening lines

Margaret went into the clock shop on a cold afternoon when the sky looked like it had been washed out with grey paint. The shop belonged to her uncle, who had died recently. She was there to clean it out — the solicitor had told her everything now belonged to her, though she had not visited the shop in years.

Shelves lined the walls, filled with clocks of every size. Most of them were stopped, their hands frozen in different directions. Then she noticed it — one clock was still ticking. She reached out and touched the glass. It was warm. That made no sense. Inside the clock's back panel, instead of a mechanism, there was a hollow space. And inside that space was a letter.

Editing Issues Identified

  • The opening lacked tension and immediacy.
  • Too much scene-setting before the mystery began.
  • Margaret's grief was stated rather than felt.
  • The ticking clock needed to feel more unsettling, sooner.
  • The ending needed a stronger final beat.

Editing Work

I opened on the wrongness of the ticking clock instead of the weather, tightened the pacing through the shop, deepened Margaret's grief through specific detail, and extended the ending into a genuinely unsettling final beat — while preserving the author's story and voice.

Edited Manuscript

After editing — the same opening, revised

Margaret knew something was wrong the moment she stepped into the clock shop. It was not the silence that unsettled her — she had expected silence. What disturbed her was the absence of stillness within that silence. Because one clock was still alive. Tick. Tick. Tick. The sound cut through the air like a heartbeat that did not belong to the room.

Every clock in the shop was frozen at the same hour — the hour of her uncle's death. She followed the ticking to a plain, unremarkable clock at the back, reached out, and touched the glass. Warm. Glass was not supposed to be warm, not in a room that had been empty for nearly a week. Inside its false mechanism, she found a folded letter with her name written on the front, in handwriting she recognized immediately as her uncle's.

The letter held only one sentence: If you are reading this, they have finally found me. Before she could react, she heard the shop door open behind her, slowly and deliberately — though the bell above it never made a sound. She turned. Someone was standing in the darkness between the shelves.
